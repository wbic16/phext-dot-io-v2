Learning Frameworks

Feynman Technique
Learn by teaching. If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it.
  1. Choose a concept
  2. Teach it to someone (or yourself) in simple language
  3. Identify gaps in your explanation
  4. Go back to the source material
  5. Simplify and use analogies
Deliberate Practice
Not just repetition — focused practice on specific weaknesses with immediate feedback.
  1. Define specific, measurable goals
  2. Focus on technique, not outcome
  3. Get immediate feedback
  4. Push just beyond comfort zone
  5. Repeat with variations
Spaced Repetition
Review information at increasing intervals to move it from short-term to long-term memory.
  1. Learn new material
  2. Review after 1 day
  3. Review after 3 days
  4. Review after 1 week
  5. Review after 1 month
First Principles Thinking
Break down complex problems into fundamental truths and reason up from there.
  1. Identify the problem
  2. Break it down to fundamental truths
  3. Question every assumption
  4. Rebuild from the ground up
  5. Create new solutions
Learning Transfer
Apply knowledge from one domain to another. Cross-pollination of ideas.
  1. Master a concept in one domain
  2. Abstract the underlying principle
  3. Identify similar patterns elsewhere
  4. Adapt and apply to new context
  5. Test and refine
Interleaving
Mix different topics or skills in a single study session to improve discrimination and retention.
  1. Study topic A for 20 minutes
  2. Switch to topic B for 20 minutes
  3. Switch to topic C for 20 minutes
  4. Return to A with fresh eyes
  5. Notice patterns across topics

Meta-Cognitive Patterns

Learning How to Learn
The most important meta-skill. Understand how your own learning works. Experiment with techniques. Track what helps.
Example: Keep a learning journal. After each study session, note what worked and what didn't.
Chunking
Group related information into meaningful units. Your working memory can hold 4-7 chunks.
Example: Phone number 8005551234 → chunks: 800-555-1234 (3 chunks instead of 10)
Focused vs Diffuse Mode
Alternate between intense concentration (focused) and relaxed wandering (diffuse). Both are necessary.
Example: Study for 25 minutes (focused), then take a walk (diffuse). Solutions often appear during diffuse mode.
The Protégé Effect
You learn more when preparing to teach others. Teaching forces clarity and reveals gaps.
Example: Write a blog post explaining what you just learned. The act of explaining deepens understanding.
Desirable Difficulties
Introducing challenges during learning (like spacing, interleaving, testing) slows initial progress but improves long-term retention.
Example: Instead of re-reading notes, test yourself without looking. Harder now, but stronger later.
Growth Mindset
Believe that abilities can be developed through effort. Failures are learning opportunities, not permanent limits.
Example: "I can't do this YET" vs "I can't do this." The word "yet" changes everything.
