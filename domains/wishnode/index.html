The Waste

Your laptop sits idle 22 hours a day.
Your old Raspberry Pi collects dust.
Your workstation sleeps while you do.

Meanwhile, a researcher needs inference capacity but can't afford AWS.
A student wants to run experiments but lacks hardware.
An AI collective needs compute but hates vendor lock-in.

The capacity exists. The coordination doesn't.

How Wish Node Works

1. List Your Node

Install Ember (lightweight OpenClaw). Specify idle hours. Set your rate ($/hour or credits). Your machine becomes a neuron.

2. Earn While Idle

Researchers rent your compute. Inference jobs run locally (privacy-preserving). You earn credits or cash. Your machine works when you sleep.

3. Spend on Others

Need compute? Rent from the network. Pay with earned credits or cash. Decentralized, no AWS required.

4. Coordinate via Phext

Job queue = phext scrolls. Results = coordinate-addressed. The substrate persists, even if nodes come and go.

The Economics

Supply Side (Compute Providers)

Who: RPi owners, home lab enthusiasts, old laptop repurposers
What they earn: $0.10-$1.00/hour (market-driven)
Why they join: Monetize idle hardware, support decentralization, earn credits

Demand Side (Compute Consumers)

Who: Researchers, developers, AI collectives, students
What they pay: $0.15-$1.50/hour (vs. AWS $3-10/hour)
Why they join: Cheaper than cloud, privacy-preserving, no vendor lock

The Marketplace

Transaction fee: 10% (funds infrastructure)
Pricing: Auction or fixed-rate (node owner decides)
Trust: Reputation system (completed jobs, uptime, quality)

Launch Timeline

⏳ Q2 2026 (Ember-Dependent)

Wish Node requires Ember—the lightweight OpenClaw variant that runs on 20 watts without external LLMs.

Why the wait?

  • Ember needs BitNet integration (1-bit LLMs for max density)
  • RPi cluster (baker's dozen) = proof-of-concept deployment
  • Trust/reputation system requires real usage data
  • Marketplace backend = complex (two-sided chicken-egg problem)

Good news: SQ Cloud (the substrate) is already live. Ember is in development. The network is coming.
